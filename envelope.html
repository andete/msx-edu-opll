2 Envelope

A tone that snaps on and off is a buzzer. What makes an operator a voice is its ADSR envelope: it Attacks to full level, Decays to a Sustain, holds while the key is down, then Releases. Every operator has its own — and on the carrier, the envelope is the note's loudness over time.

Hold a key and watch the dot ride the curve. Two OPLL quirks show up here: the Key-On damp (the level first dips toward silence before the attack climbs, so a re-keyed note doesn't click), and EG-type — a sustained operator (EG 1) holds at the sustain level forever, while a percussive one (EG 0) keeps decaying to nothing.

The two step-sizes, and Key Scaling

The envelope lives in the attenuation domain — it counts down toward loud and up toward silence, in steps of 0.375 dB. Key Scaling of Rate (KSR) makes higher notes run their envelopes faster, the way a real string's high notes decay quicker than its low ones — press a high key and a low one and watch the attack speed differ.
