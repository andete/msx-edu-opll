2 Envelope

An operator on its own just switches on and off. Its ADSR envelope is what turns that into a voice: it Attacks to full level, Decays to a Sustain, holds while the key is down, then Releases. Every operator has its own; on the carrier, the envelope is the note's loudness over time.

Hold a key and watch the dot ride the curve. Two OPLL quirks show up here: the Key-On damp (the level first dips toward silence before the attack climbs, so a re-keyed note doesn't click), and EG-type: a sustained operator (EG 1) holds at the sustain level for as long as the key is down, while a percussive one (EG 0) keeps decaying to nothing.

The two step-sizes, and Key Scaling

The envelope lives in the attenuation domain: it counts down toward loud and up toward silence, in steps of 0.375 dB. Key Scaling of Rate (KSR) makes higher notes run their envelopes faster, the way a real string's high notes decay quicker than its low ones. Press a high key and a low one and watch the attack speed differ.

Slow release (channel SUS) is the odd one out: every other control here belongs to the instrument, but SUS is a bit in the channel's own register (2x bit 5), so it applies to whatever patch the channel is playing. It does nothing while a key is held — it only takes over at Key-Off, where the note releases at one fixed slow rate and the operator's RR is ignored. Tick it and the R segment above redraws to the rate it will actually use.
